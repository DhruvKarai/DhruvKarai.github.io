Glossary

The medicine words used on this site, explained in plain English.

A B C D E H I L M N O P R S T U W

A

Anaphylaxis
A severe, sudden allergic reaction. It is an emergency: call 999.
Angioedema
Sudden swelling under the skin, often of the face, lips, tongue or throat. It can be serious. Get urgent medical help if it happens.
Anticoagulant
A medicine that makes blood take longer to clot. Often called a blood thinner.
Antihistamine
A medicine that blocks histamine, the chemical behind many allergy symptoms.
Asthma
A long-term condition where the airways become inflamed and narrow, causing wheeze, cough and breathlessness.

B

BNF
British National Formulary. The main UK reference on how medicines are used.

C

Cholesterol
A fatty substance in the blood. Too much of the 'bad' (LDL) kind raises the risk of heart disease and stroke.
Contraindicated
Should not be used, because it could cause harm.

D

Diuretic
A 'water tablet'. It makes the kidneys pass more salt and water as urine.

E

eMC
The electronic medicines compendium. A UK website with the official leaflets and SmPCs for licensed medicines.
Enzyme
A protein that speeds up a chemical reaction in the body.

H

Half-life
The time it takes for the amount of a medicine in the body to fall by half.

I

Inhibitor
A medicine that blocks or slows down something, usually an enzyme.
INR
A blood test that shows how long your blood takes to clot. It is used to check how warfarin is working.

L

Lactic acidosis
A rare, serious build-up of lactic acid in the blood.

M

Metabolised
Broken down by the body, mostly in the liver.

N

NSAID
Short for non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drug. A group of painkillers, including ibuprofen and naproxen, that also reduce inflammation and fever.

O

Oedema
Swelling caused by fluid building up in the body, often in the ankles.

P

Platelets
Tiny blood cell fragments that stick together to plug a damaged blood vessel and start a clot.
Prostaglandins
Chemical messengers the body makes. They help cause pain, swelling and fever, and have other jobs too, such as protecting the stomach lining.

R

Receptor
A docking point on a cell. A chemical messenger or a medicine fits onto it like a key in a lock and changes what the cell does.
Reflux
Stomach acid coming back up into the gullet, which causes heartburn.
Renal
To do with the kidneys.
Rhabdomyolysis
Severe muscle breakdown that can harm the kidneys.

S

Serotonin syndrome
A rare but serious reaction when too much serotonin builds up. Signs include agitation, sweating, shaking and fever. It needs urgent medical help.
SmPC
Summary of Product Characteristics. The official, detailed information for healthcare professionals that goes with each licensed medicine.
Statin
A medicine that lowers cholesterol by reducing how much the liver makes.

T

Thyroid
A small gland in the neck that makes hormones controlling how the body uses energy.

U

Ulcer
A sore on the lining of the stomach or gut.

W

Withdrawal
Unpleasant symptoms that can happen when a medicine is stopped suddenly after regular use.
Information only, not medical advice. These are simplified definitions. Speak to a pharmacist, doctor or NHS 111 about your own health and medicines.
