Cetirizine

Zirtek, Piriteze and Benadryl Allergy

Antihistamine: an allergy medicine that blocks histamine

Key points

  • An antihistamine that eases allergy symptoms such as hay fever, hives and itchy skin.
  • It blocks the effects of histamine, a chemical your body releases when you meet something you are allergic to.
  • NHS calls it a non-drowsy antihistamine, but it can still make some people sleepy. Do not drive if it does.
  • Adults usually take one tablet once a day. The dose is usually lower for people with kidney problems.

The details

How it works

Cetirizine is a selective blocker of histamine H1 receptors on cells outside the brain. When you meet an allergen, your body releases histamine, which causes itching, sneezing and a runny nose. By blocking the receptor that histamine acts on, cetirizine reduces those effects.

How quickly

NHS says cetirizine starts to work within an hour. The SmPC says blood levels peak about one hour after a dose, and the medicine has a half-life of about 10 hours.

Doses

Adults and children aged 12 and over: 10mg once a day, as tablets or liquid. Under-12s usually take a lower dose, so check the packet. Doses are usually lower for people with kidney problems, and the SmPC says it should not be used in end-stage kidney disease on dialysis. These are typical adult values; if your packet or prescriber says something different, follow that. These are typical adult doses, not personal advice.

Side effects

The most common effect is feeling sleepy or tired. Other common effects are headache, dry mouth, feeling sick, dizziness and diarrhoea.

Get help if: you bruise more easily than normal, bleeding takes longer to stop, or you have signs of a serious allergic reaction (anaphylaxis). See the NHS page for the full list.

Extra care
  • Kidney problems: NHS says tell a doctor or pharmacist if you have kidney failure. The SmPC lists lower doses for reduced kidney function.
  • Sleepiness: NHS says do not drive, cycle or use machines if it makes you sleepy or dizzy.
  • Other medicines that cause drowsiness, dry mouth or difficulty peeing: NHS says check first, because cetirizine may make these worse.
  • Food additives: NHS says tell a doctor or pharmacist if you have an allergy to food additives.
  • Epilepsy: BNF lists epilepsy as a condition needing caution with cetirizine.
Common mistakes
  • Taking two different antihistamines together unless a doctor tells you to.
  • Assuming "non-drowsy" means it can never cause sleepiness.
  • Giving the adult tablet to a young child. Children have age-specific doses.
  • Stopping long-term use suddenly. NHS says there is a small chance of severe itching if you do.
Pregnancy

NHS says cetirizine can be taken in pregnancy. There is not a lot of information, but nothing suggests it harms the baby. When breastfeeding, NHS says cetirizine is one of the best antihistamine choices because only very small amounts get into breast milk. The SmPC advises caution in both. BNF says most manufacturers advise avoiding antihistamines in pregnancy and while breastfeeding, although there is no evidence they cause birth defects.
