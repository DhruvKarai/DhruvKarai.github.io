Omeprazole

Losec

Proton pump inhibitor (PPI): reduces stomach acid
What it is forStomach ulcers, heartburn, acid reflux (GORD), and, together with antibiotics, Helicobacter pylori stomach infection.

Key points

  • Reduces the amount of acid your stomach makes. Used for heartburn, reflux and stomach ulcers.
  • Shop-bought omeprazole is for adults over 18. Do not take it for longer than 2 weeks without seeing a doctor.
  • It can make it harder to notice symptoms of serious conditions such as stomach cancer, so see a GP if your symptoms get worse or do not go away.
  • Swallow coated tablets and capsules whole with water.

The details

How it works

Cells in the stomach lining make acid using an enzyme system called the proton pump. Omeprazole blocks that pump, so the stomach makes much less acid. That gives ulcers and an irritated food pipe time to heal.

How quickly

Omeprazole is absorbed quickly, and blood levels peak about 1 to 2 hours after a dose. Taken once a day, its full acid-lowering effect is reached within about 4 days. The SmPC says most duodenal ulcers heal within 2 weeks of treatment.

Doses

A typical adult dose is 20mg once a day, and the SmPC gives 40mg once a day for some conditions. NHS says it is usually taken once or twice a day, at the same time each morning (and evening if twice a day), with or without food. Swallow with water and do not chew or crush coated tablets or capsules. How long you take it depends on why you take it. These are typical adult values; if your packet or prescriber says something different, follow that. These are typical adult doses, not personal advice.

Side effects

Headache, stomach pain, feeling or being sick, constipation or diarrhoea, and more wind.

Get help if: you notice side effects that bother you or do not go away. See the NHS page for serious effects.

Extra care
  • Allergy: do not use it if you have had an allergic reaction or a serious side effect from omeprazole or another PPI.
  • Kidney or liver conditions, and osteoporosis (weak bones): tell a doctor or pharmacist first.
  • Long use: the risk of serious effects such as very low vitamin B12 and weaker bones is higher after a year or more.
  • Tests: it can affect some blood tests, checks for some tumours and gastroscopy. You may need to stop it for a few days or weeks beforehand.
  • Other medicines: NHS lists lansoprazole, famotidine, digoxin, warfarin, clopidogrel, diazepam, phenytoin, St John's Wort, nelfinavir and atazanavir. The SmPC says omeprazole must not be used with nelfinavir.
  • Alcohol: it is best not to drink alcohol, because it can increase stomach acid and make symptoms worse.
Common mistakes
  • Chewing or crushing coated tablets or capsules. They are coated so the acid does not destroy the medicine.
  • Taking shop-bought omeprazole for longer than 2 weeks without seeing a doctor.
  • Ignoring symptoms that get worse, or new alarm symptoms. Omeprazole can mask signs of serious conditions.
  • Doubling up after a missed dose. Take it when you remember unless the next dose is nearly due.
  • Not mentioning omeprazole before a gastroscopy or other tests.
Pregnancy

NHS says you can usually take omeprazole in pregnancy and while breastfeeding, but check with a pharmacist or doctor first. The SmPC says studies of more than 1000 exposed pregnancies found no harm to the pregnancy or baby, and that omeprazole passes into breast milk but is not likely to affect the baby at normal doses.
