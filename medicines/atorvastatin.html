Atorvastatin

Lipitor

Statin: lowers cholesterol

Key points

  • Lowers cholesterol by making the liver produce less of it. Used to lower the risk of heart attack and stroke.
  • Usually taken once a day, long term, sometimes for life.
  • Not recommended in pregnancy. Tell a doctor if you are planning a pregnancy.
  • Report unexplained muscle pain, tenderness or weakness.

The details

How it works

The liver makes cholesterol using an enzyme called HMG-CoA reductase. Atorvastatin competes with that enzyme and slows cholesterol production, so cholesterol in the blood falls.

How quickly

The SmPC says the effect is seen within 2 weeks and is usually at its maximum within 4 weeks. A low starting dose may be increased after at least 4 weeks.

Doses

A typical adult starting dose is 10mg once a day, adjusted by a doctor at intervals of 4 weeks or more, up to a maximum of 80mg a day (SmPC). NHS says to take it once a day at the same time, with or without food. These are typical adult values; if your packet or prescriber says something different, follow that. These are typical adult doses, not personal advice.

Side effects

Headache, dizziness, feeling sick, digestive upset, muscle aches, swollen joints, nose problems and sleep problems.

Get help if: you get unexplained muscle pain or other side effects that bother you. See the NHS page.

Extra care
  • Tell a doctor about liver or kidney problems, an underactive thyroid, muscle conditions such as myasthenia gravis, a past brain bleed or heavy drinking.
  • Muscle effects: they can rarely become serious muscle damage (rhabdomyolysis). Liver blood tests are done before and during treatment (SmPC 4.4).
  • Other medicines: NHS lists fibrates, itraconazole, fluconazole, fusidic acid, erythromycin, clarithromycin, some HIV and hepatitis C medicines, diltiazem, verapamil, ciclosporin, warfarin and St John's wort.
  • Limit alcohol and grapefruit juice (NHS).
Common mistakes
  • Stopping because you feel well. Cholesterol has no symptoms, and it is usually a long-term medicine.
  • Doubling up after a missed dose. NHS says to skip it and take the next one as normal.
  • Drinking more than 1 or 2 small glasses of grapefruit juice a day. NHS says to limit it.
  • Ignoring muscle pain or weakness, especially with fever or feeling unwell.
  • Not mentioning it when starting other medicines such as some antibiotics or antifungals.
Pregnancy

NHS says atorvastatin is not recommended in pregnancy, and to stop it 3 months before trying to conceive. The SmPC lists pregnancy and breastfeeding as contraindications. NHS says to check with a doctor or pharmacist before breastfeeding.
