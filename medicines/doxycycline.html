Doxycycline

Tetracycline antibiotic

Tetracycline antibiotic: stops bacteria from multiplying

Key points

  • An antibiotic for infections such as chest infections, gum infections, some UTIs and STIs.
  • Also used for acne and rosacea, and to prevent malaria when travelling.
  • Take it with a full glass of water, sitting or standing, and avoid lying down for 30 minutes.
  • It can make skin more sensitive to the sun. Do not take it with antacids, iron or zinc supplements at the same time.

The details

How it works

Doxycycline is bacteriostatic. The SmPC says it is believed to work by inhibiting protein synthesis in bacteria, so they cannot grow and multiply. It is active against a wide range of bacteria. Antibiotics do not work against viruses such as colds and flu.

How quickly

The SmPC says doxycycline is almost completely absorbed after you swallow it. How soon you feel better depends on the infection, so take the full course as prescribed.

Doses

Doses depend on the infection and your prescriber. NHS says take it at the same time each day, swallow tablets or capsules whole with a full glass of water, sitting or standing, and avoid lying down for at least 30 minutes. Dispersible tablets are dissolved in water first. These are typical adult values; if your packet or prescriber says something different, follow that. These are typical adult doses, not personal advice.

Side effects

NHS lists headaches, feeling or being sick, and a rash as common effects.

Get help if: you have signs of a serious allergic reaction, skin that burns or rashes in the sun, stomach ache with bloody diarrhoea, or tiredness, easy bruising or getting ill a lot.

Extra care
  • Allergy: NHS says tell a doctor if you have had an allergic reaction to doxycycline or another antibiotic.
  • Liver or kidney conditions, lupus, myasthenia gravis, or alcohol dependence: NHS says tell your doctor.
  • Other medicines and supplements: NHS says antacids, indigestion remedies, iron and zinc supplements, acne medicines and blood thinners such as warfarin may not mix well with doxycycline.
  • Sun: NHS lists sun sensitivity as a possible serious side effect.
  • Alcohol: NHS says it is best not to drink, because it can stop the medicine working properly.
Common mistakes
  • Taking it just before lying down, which can irritate the throat.
  • Taking it at the same time as an antacid or iron tablet.
  • Stopping the course early because you feel better.
  • Sunbathing without protection while taking it.
Pregnancy

NHS says doxycycline is not usually used in pregnancy because it can affect how the baby's teeth develop. A doctor may recommend it if the benefits outweigh the risks, and NHS says the risks are lower early in pregnancy, within the first 13 weeks. NHS also says it is not usually used while breastfeeding, because of the risk of side effects in the baby and effects on tooth development.
