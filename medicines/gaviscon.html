Gaviscon

Heartburn and indigestion remedy

Alginate and antacid: forms a protective layer on stomach contents

Key points

  • A heartburn and indigestion remedy that forms a protective layer on top of your stomach contents.
  • You can buy it from shops and pharmacies for most people aged 12 and over.
  • Usually taken after meals and at bedtime. If you buy it, do not take it for longer than 7 days without checking with a doctor.
  • NHS says you can take paracetamol with it, but avoid ibuprofen and aspirin, which can make symptoms worse.

The details

How it works

Gaviscon works mostly by a physical effect, not by acting on cells. The SmPC for Gaviscon Advance says it reacts with stomach acid to form a raft of alginic acid gel that floats on the stomach contents. This can impede reflux for up to 4 hours and protects the oesophagus from acid, pepsin and bile. NHS says it also contains an ingredient that neutralises some stomach acid. Different Gaviscon products have different ingredients.

How quickly

NHS says Gaviscon usually starts to work soon after you take it. The SmPC says the raft can impede reflux for up to 4 hours.

Doses

NHS says for ages 12 and over the dose depends on the type: 10 to 20ml of liquid after meals and at bedtime up to 4 times a day, or 10ml four times a day for Gaviscon Advance, or 2 to 4 tablets up to 4 times a day. Chew the tablets, do not swallow them whole. Always follow the packet, as products differ. Under-12s only on a doctor's advice. These are typical adult values; if your packet or prescriber says something different, follow that. These are typical adult doses, not personal advice.

Side effects

NHS says Gaviscon is a very safe medicine and most people have no side effects. In rare cases some types may cause feeling sick, vomiting, constipation or diarrhoea.

Get help if: your lips, mouth, throat or tongue suddenly swell, you struggle to breathe or swallow, or you have signs of anaphylaxis. Talk to a doctor if you still have symptoms after 7 days.

Extra care
  • Low calcium or low salt diets: NHS says check with your doctor or pharmacist first.
  • Kidney, liver or heart disease, low phosphate, or phenylketonuria: NHS says check first.
  • Under 12s: NHS says only give it if prescribed or recommended by a doctor.
  • Other medicines: NHS says some medicines can affect how Gaviscon works, so ask a pharmacist.
  • Pain relief: NHS says avoid ibuprofen and aspirin, which can make symptoms worse.
Common mistakes
  • Swallowing the chewable tablets whole.
  • Taking it for more than 7 days without checking with a doctor.
  • Taking more than the dose on the packet.
  • Taking ibuprofen or aspirin for pain when you have heartburn.
Pregnancy

NHS says you can take Gaviscon in pregnancy, but follow the instructions and do not take more than recommended. If it is not working, ask your pharmacist or doctor. NHS says you can take it while breastfeeding, and it should not cause side effects in your baby.
