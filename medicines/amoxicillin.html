Amoxicillin

Amoxil

Penicillin antibiotic (a beta-lactam antibiotic)

Key points

  • A penicillin antibiotic for bacterial infections. It is prescription only.
  • Usually taken 3 times a day. Finish the course even if you feel better.
  • It does not work on viruses, so it will not help colds or flu.
  • Tell a doctor before starting if you have ever had an allergic reaction to penicillin.

The details

How it works

Amoxicillin kills bacteria by stopping them building their cell walls. Without a proper wall the bacteria burst. It has no effect on viruses, so it does not help with colds or flu.

How quickly

Amoxicillin is absorbed well from the gut, and blood levels peak about 1 hour after a dose. NHS says most infections start to improve within a few days. Tell your doctor if you are not better after 3 to 5 days, or if you get worse at any point.

Doses

Capsules are usually 250mg to 500mg, taken 3 times a day and spaced evenly through the day. Swallow them whole with water, before or after food. A liquid is available for children and for people who cannot swallow capsules, and children's doses are lower. These are typical adult values; if your packet or prescriber says something different, follow that. These are typical adult doses, not personal advice.

Side effects

Feeling sick and diarrhoea are the most common. A skin rash can happen, and about 1 in 10 people have an allergic reaction. Thrush is possible.

Get help if: you have bloody or severe diarrhoea, yellow skin or eyes, unusual bruising, or signs of a serious allergy such as a swollen face or throat or trouble breathing (call 999).

Extra care
  • Allergy: tell a doctor before starting if you have ever had a reaction to amoxicillin, penicillin or any other medicine.
  • Also tell them about liver or kidney problems, glandular fever (amoxicillin can cause a rash with it), recent or upcoming vaccinations, and upcoming blood or urine tests, which amoxicillin can affect.
  • Other medicines: check with a pharmacist if you take methotrexate, warfarin, probenecid, allopurinol or other antibiotics.
  • Contraception: amoxicillin does not stop contraception working, but being sick or having severe watery diarrhoea, or any diarrhoea lasting over 24 hours, can stop pills working.
  • Thrush can happen after a course of antibiotics. Speak to a pharmacist or doctor if you think you have it.
Common mistakes
  • Stopping early because you feel better. The infection could come back.
  • Doubling up after a missed dose. Take it when you remember unless the next dose is nearly due, and never take 2 doses at once.
  • Asking for it for a cold or flu. Those are viruses and amoxicillin does not work on them.
  • Measuring liquid with a kitchen teaspoon. Use the syringe or spoon that comes with it.
  • Not mentioning a past reaction to penicillin before starting.
Pregnancy

NHS says it is safe to take amoxicillin in pregnancy, but check with a pharmacist or doctor first. It is also OK while breastfeeding because only tiny amounts reach breast milk. A few babies have had mild effects such as diarrhoea, drowsiness or a rash, but this is very rare. Contact a healthcare professional or 111 if your baby feeds less well, is sick, has diarrhoea, thrush or a rash, or seems unusually sleepy. NHS says there is no evidence that it affects fertility.
