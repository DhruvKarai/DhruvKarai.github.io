Loratadine

Clarityn

Antihistamine: an allergy medicine that blocks histamine

Key points

  • A non-drowsy antihistamine that eases allergy symptoms such as hay fever, hives and itchy eyes.
  • It blocks the effects of histamine, a chemical the body releases during an allergic reaction.
  • NHS says it is much less likely to make you sleepy than some other antihistamines, but some people still feel sleepy.
  • Adults usually take one 10mg tablet once a day. You can buy it from pharmacies and shops.

The details

How it works

Loratadine is a tricyclic antihistamine with selective activity at peripheral H1 receptors, the receptors histamine acts on outside the brain. Blocking them reduces itching, sneezing and runny nose. The SmPC says it has no clinically significant sedative or anticholinergic effects in most people at the recommended dose.

How quickly

NHS says you should start to feel better within 1 to 3 hours of taking loratadine. The SmPC says it is rapidly and well absorbed.

Doses

NHS says the usual adult dose is 10mg once a day, as tablets (10mg) or liquid. Doses are usually lower for people with liver problems, and may be taken every other day. For children the dose depends on weight or age, so follow the packet or your doctor. These are typical adult values; if your packet or prescriber says something different, follow that. These are typical adult doses, not personal advice.

Side effects

NHS says the most common side effect is feeling sleepy, in more than 1 in 100 people. In children, headaches and feeling tired or nervous can happen.

Get help if: your lips, mouth, throat or tongue suddenly swell, you struggle to breathe or swallow, or you have signs of anaphylaxis. NHS says call 999 for these.

Extra care
  • Liver problems: NHS says tell your doctor or pharmacist if you have severe liver failure.
  • Epilepsy or seizure risk: NHS says tell your doctor or pharmacist.
  • Allergy tests: NHS says loratadine may affect the results, so you may need to stop it a few days before.
  • Food additives: NHS says tell a doctor or pharmacist if you have an allergy to food additives.
  • Alcohol: NHS says it is best not to drink, because it can make you feel sleepy.
Common mistakes
  • Assuming "non-drowsy" means it can never cause sleepiness.
  • Taking two antihistamines together unless a doctor says to.
  • Driving or cycling if you feel sleepy.
  • Forgetting to mention it before an allergy test.
Pregnancy

NHS says you can take loratadine in pregnancy. NHS also says if you need an antihistamine while breastfeeding, loratadine is one of the best choices.
