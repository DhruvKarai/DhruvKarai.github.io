Clarithromycin

Macrolide antibiotic

Macrolide antibiotic: stops bacteria making proteins

Key points

  • A macrolide antibiotic that stops the growth of the bacteria causing an infection.
  • Used for chest infections such as pneumonia, skin problems such as cellulitis, ear infections, and Helicobacter pylori. Prescription only.
  • NHS says it is sometimes used by people who are allergic to penicillin and similar antibiotics such as amoxicillin.
  • Finish the full course, even if you feel better. It interacts with several medicines, including statins and warfarin.

The details

How it works

Clarithromycin is a macrolide antibiotic. The SmPC says it binds to the 50S ribosomal subunit of susceptible bacteria and so inhibits their protein synthesis. Without the proteins they need, the bacteria cannot grow. NHS says macrolide antibiotics work by stopping the growth of the bacteria that cause the infection. Antibiotics do not work against viruses.

How quickly

NHS says that for most infections you should feel better within a few days, but you should finish the full course. If you do not feel better after 7 or 10 days (depending on the infection), or feel worse at any point, tell your doctor.

Doses

NHS says the usual dose is 250mg to 500mg twice a day, although the dose may be lower for children. Slow-release tablets are usually 500mg once a day. Take it at the same time each day, swallow tablets whole with water, with or without food. The SmPC gives 250mg twice a day for respiratory and skin infections, increased to 500mg twice a day in severe infections, usually for 6 to 14 days. Your prescriber sets the dose and length. These are typical adult values; if your packet or prescriber says something different, follow that. These are typical adult doses, not personal advice.

Side effects

NHS lists feeling or being sick, diarrhoea, bloating and indigestion, headaches and difficulty sleeping as common effects.

Get help if: you have a faster or irregular heartbeat, pale poo, dark pee or yellow skin or eyes, severe stomach or back pain, diarrhoea that contains blood or mucus, hallucinations, or signs of a serious allergic reaction.

Extra care
  • Heart problems: NHS says tell your doctor if you have heart problems, irregular heartbeats, or low potassium or magnesium.
  • Myasthenia gravis: NHS says clarithromycin can make symptoms worse.
  • Liver or kidney problems and past antibiotic-related diarrhoea: NHS says tell your doctor.
  • Other medicines: NHS says check with a pharmacist if you take epilepsy medicines, theophylline, colchicine, digoxin, warfarin, ticagrelor, statins such as simvastatin or atorvastatin, midazolam or domperidone.
  • St John's wort: NHS says it may not mix well.
Common mistakes
  • Stopping early because you feel better. NHS says the infection could come back.
  • Taking it with a statin or warfarin without checking.
  • Chewing or breaking the tablets.
  • Using a kitchen spoon to measure the liquid instead of the syringe or spoon supplied.
Pregnancy

NHS says clarithromycin can be taken in pregnancy, but other antibiotics may be more suitable, so ask your doctor or pharmacist. NHS says you can take it while breastfeeding if your baby is healthy, after talking to your doctor or pharmacist, because very small amounts pass into breast milk.
