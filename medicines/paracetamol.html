Key points

  • A painkiller and fever reducer you can buy in shops and pharmacies.
  • Never take more than the packet says. Many cold and flu remedies also contain paracetamol.
  • Do not use it for more than 3 days unless a doctor tells you to.
  • Usually the painkiller of choice in pregnancy and while breastfeeding.

The details

How it works

Paracetamol relieves pain and brings down a high temperature, but it does little against swelling. Exactly how it works is not fully understood. It is thought to act mainly in the brain and spinal cord by reducing the production of prostaglandins, chemicals involved in pain and fever.

How quickly

Paracetamol is absorbed quickly from the gut. Levels in the blood peak about 30 minutes to 2 hours after a dose.

Doses

Typical adult packs say 1 or 2 tablets of 500mg, at least 4 hours apart, with no more than 8 tablets in 24 hours. You may need a lower dose if you weigh under 50kg. These are typical adult values; if your packet or prescriber says something different, follow that. These are typical adult doses, not personal advice.

Side effects

Side effects are very rare when it is taken as the packet says.

Get help if: allergic reaction (swollen throat or tongue, itchy raised rash, trouble breathing), signs of liver problems (feeling sick, yellow skin or eyes), or unusual bruising and nosebleeds. Call NHS 111 if you think you have serious side effects, or if you have taken more than the packet says.

Extra care
  • Taking too much can damage the liver. Many cold and flu remedies also contain paracetamol, so check labels.
  • Extra care with liver or kidney conditions or heavy drinking.
  • Can interact with warfarin, flucloxacillin and some anti-sickness medicines.
Common mistakes
  • Taking it together with a cold and flu remedy that also contains paracetamol.
  • Taking extra for stronger pain relief. More does not work better and can harm the liver.
  • Carrying on for more than 3 days without checking with a doctor.
  • Giving a child adult tablets. Children's doses are different, so use children's medicine and its own instructions.
Pregnancy

If you are pregnant, paracetamol is usually the best painkiller to use, and NHS says it is commonly taken in pregnancy and does not harm your baby. It is also usually the best choice while breastfeeding, and only tiny amounts reach breast milk. Ask a pharmacist or doctor if you have any worries.
