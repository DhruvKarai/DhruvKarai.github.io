Amlodipine

Key points

  • Relaxes and widens blood vessels. Used for high blood pressure and angina (chest pain from the heart).
  • Taken once a day at the same time, with or without food.
  • Do not stop it suddenly without talking to a doctor.
  • A common effect is swelling of the ankles or feet.

The details

How it works

Amlodipine reduces the amount of calcium moving into the muscle cells in blood vessel walls. The vessels relax and widen, so blood flows more easily and blood pressure falls.

How quickly

The SmPC says blood levels peak 6 to 12 hours after a dose and the half-life is about 35 to 50 hours, which is why once a day works. Food does not affect absorption.

Doses

A typical adult starting dose is 5mg once a day, up to a maximum of 10mg once a day (SmPC). NHS says to take it at the same time each day, before or after food. These are typical adult values; if your packet or prescriber says something different, follow that. These are typical adult doses, not personal advice.

Side effects

Swollen ankles, feet or legs, headache, dizziness, sleepiness, a fast or noticeable heartbeat, flushing, tummy pain, feeling sick, constipation or diarrhoea, and tiredness.

Get help if: you feel dizzy or sleepy (do not drive) or side effects do not ease. See the NHS page.

Extra care
  • May not suit people with low blood pressure, heart failure, a recent heart attack or narrowed heart valves.
  • Other medicines: NHS lists simvastatin (the SmPC limits simvastatin to 20mg a day with amlodipine), rifampicin, other blood pressure medicines, fluconazole, some HIV medicines, ciclosporin, tacrolimus and St John's wort.
  • Alcohol can lower blood pressure further. NHS says to limit it.
  • Serious but rare: allergic reaction, pancreatitis, severe skin reactions. See the NHS page.
Common mistakes
  • Stopping because you feel fine. High blood pressure often has no symptoms.
  • Doubling up after a missed dose. NHS says to skip it and take the next one as usual.
  • Eating grapefruit. NHS says to avoid it.
  • Ignoring ankle or foot swelling instead of mentioning it.
  • Getting up quickly if you feel dizzy, especially with alcohol.
Pregnancy

NHS says amlodipine is not usually recommended in pregnancy because safer medicines exist, and is not usually used while breastfeeding. The SmPC says to use it in pregnancy only if there is no safer alternative, and that it passes into breast milk with unknown effects on babies.
