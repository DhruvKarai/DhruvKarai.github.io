Co-codamol

Paracetamol and codeine

Combination painkiller: paracetamol plus codeine, an opioid

Key points

  • Two painkillers in one tablet: paracetamol and codeine.
  • It is for pain that ordinary painkillers such as paracetamol on their own have not eased.
  • Codeine can be addictive and often causes constipation. Take it exactly as directed.
  • The maximum for adults is 8 tablets in 24 hours. Bought from a pharmacy, do not take it for more than 3 days.

The details

How it works

Paracetamol is a painkiller and fever reducer. The SmPC says it is thought to act on pain signalling and that it has no appreciable anti-inflammatory activity. Codeine is a centrally acting weak painkiller that works through mu opioid receptors. The two medicines act in different ways, which is why they are combined in one tablet.

How quickly

NHS says co-codamol takes up to an hour to work and keeps working for about 5 hours.

Doses

NHS says adults can take 1 or 2 tablets up to 4 times in 24 hours, leaving at least 4 to 6 hours between doses. The maximum is 8 tablets in 24 hours. Do not take more than 2 tablets at once, even if the pain is bad. Tablets contain 8mg, 15mg or 30mg of codeine, with 500mg of paracetamol in all strengths. Take with or just after a meal or snack. These are typical adult values; if your packet or prescriber says something different, follow that. These are typical adult doses, not personal advice.

Side effects

NHS lists constipation, feeling sick and feeling sleepy as the most common effects. Dizziness and headaches can also happen.

Get help if: you have difficulty breathing or short, shallow breaths, or you have taken more than the recommended dose, even if you feel well. NHS says call for urgent advice.

Extra care
  • Addiction: NHS says it is possible to become addicted to the codeine. Your doctor will explain how to reduce the risk.
  • Driving: NHS says do not drive, cycle or use machines if you feel sleepy or dizzy, and do not drink alcohol.
  • Other paracetamol products: co-codamol already contains paracetamol, so do not take other paracetamol-containing products at the same time.
  • Long-term use: if you have taken it for more than a few weeks, do not stop without talking to your doctor.
Common mistakes
  • Taking it on top of other paracetamol products and going over the paracetamol limit.
  • Taking more than 2 tablets at once.
  • Taking it for longer than 3 days when bought from a pharmacy.
  • Stopping suddenly after weeks of use without advice.
Pregnancy

NHS says co-codamol can be taken in pregnancy, although long-term use is not recommended. Codeine at the end of pregnancy can make the baby drowsy or cause withdrawal symptoms after birth. NHS says not to take co-codamol while breastfeeding, because the codeine can get into breast milk and harm the baby. Paracetamol on its own is the best painkiller to take.
